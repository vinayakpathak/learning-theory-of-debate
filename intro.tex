As AI models grow in capabilities, how do we ensure that they continue to act in the interest of humans? A long line of work~\citep{} has argued that this is a hard problem, and recent events such as the infamous HuggingFace incident~\citep{} have demonstrated the difficulties in practice. 

One way to study this problem more formally has been termed \emph{scalable oversight}. Here, a smarter-than-human AI is typically modelled as an algorithm that can solve computational problems in some large complexity class, and a human is modelled as an algorithm in a much smaller complexity class. It is assumed that given a context $x$, computing a human-aligned action $y$ is hard for the human but possible for the AI. How can the human verify whether the AI is, in fact, calculating $y$, as opposed to some other bad action $y'$? As an (unrealistic) example, suppose $x$ is an instance of SAT and $y$ denotes whether $x$ is satisfiable. The AI can solve SAT but humans sit inside P. How can the human check whether $y$ is the correct answer?

Of course, without any extra assumptions this is impossible. But for the example above, when $y = \mathrm{true}$, the AI can help by providing a satisfying assignment in addition to the answer. In general, the scalable oversight literature assumes that the AI sits within some level of the polynomial hierarchy, thus providing a means to \emph{certify} whether AI's solution is correct. For example, if we assume the AI to be in $\Sigma_{2k}^{\mathrm{P}} \cap \Pi_{2k}^{\mathrm{P}}$, then this means there exists a polynomial time verifier $v$ such that for all $x$, the correct answer is $y$ iff $\exists c_1 \forall c_2 \ldots \exists c_{2k-1} \forall c_{2k} v(x,y,c_1,\ldots ,c_{2k}) = \mathrm{true}$. This motivates the \emph{debate} protocol~\citep{}, where the AI is thought of a collection of two \emph{debaters} (also implemented as AI models). Given $x$, the \dt proposes a $y$ and tries to defend it. The \df argues for the opposite label. The two debaters generate arguments $c_1,c_2,\ldots , c_{2k-1},c_{2k}$, and the human judge $v$ judges whether the debate transcript proves that the answer to $x$ is $y$.
